## Module missions

### 1. Breath, Alignment & Vocal Health

Mission: establish a repeatable physical setup that supports safe singing. Lessons cover whole-body alignment, low-release breath, and a reusable readiness routine. The video brief demonstrates side/front alignment, silent inhalation, and a short reset. Technique cues emphasize unlocked knees, released shoulders, and easy airflow; common mistakes include raised shoulders, locked posture, and forcing the inhale. The self-check asks whether the student can reset without throat tension. ARIA may compare setup evidence and prompt reflection, but cannot complete the lesson or award XP.

The +50 real-world practice is a before/after breath comparison. The +50 Lab practice is a breath-cycle evidence check using a real assigned Academy tool. The 350 Core XP challenge is a short supported vocal performance. Rubric: alignment 20, breath coordination 25, phrase control 20, healthy production 20, preparation/completion 15; passing score 70.

### 2. Find Your Natural Voice

Mission: connect comfortable speech to an unforced singing sound. Lessons cover speech baseline, speech-to-pitch slides, and natural tone in a short phrase. The video brief compares spoken, over-manufactured, and connected versions of one phrase. Cues emphasize conversational intention and a released jaw; mistakes include imitating another singer and adding unnecessary volume. The practices compare authentic tone choices, and the 350 XP challenge is a natural-voice performance study. Rubric: technique 30, accuracy 25, expression 30, completion 15; pass 70.

### 3. Pitch Control

Mission: hear, match, assess, and correct pitch without panic. Lessons cover reference-note listening, stable sustain, simple intervals, sharp/flat tendencies, and short-pattern correction. The video brief models the listen–breathe–sing–check loop. The +50 field task compares a first and final melody take; the +50 Lab task compares reference/response attempts with an assigned tool. The Core challenge is a short melodic performance. Rubric: pitch matching 25, sustained pitch 20, melodic accuracy 25, self-correction 15, prepared take 15; pass 70.

### 4. Rhythm, Timing & Groove

Mission: sing confidently in time with music. Lessons cover steady pulse, entrances, note duration, and groove. The video brief models pulse, counted entrances, clapped rhythm, and straight versus syncopated phrases. Practices lock a section to a backing track and compare rhythm evidence. The Core challenge is `Lock Into the Groove`. Rubric: pulse 25, entrances/releases 20, duration 20, phrasing 20, completion 15; pass 70.

### 5. Tone & Resonance

Mission: explore resonance and tone color without forcing. Lessons use gentle hum-to-vowel exploration, bright/dark/balanced comparisons, and lyric-driven tone choices. Practices compare three labeled phrase colors. The Core challenge selects a repeatable color that serves the lyric. Rubric: tone consistency 20, intentional color 25, healthy resonance 20, low tension 20, lyric fit 15; pass 70.

### 6. Diction & Storytelling

Mission: turn written lyrics into specific communication. Lessons cover lyric evidence, intention choices, and phrase shaping. The video brief annotates a lyric and demonstrates how intention changes emphasis without changing pitch. Practices test meaning with a listener and rehearse contrasting intentions. The Core challenge is a narrative verse performance. Rubric: storytelling 35, phrasing 25, vocal technique 20, completion 20; pass 70. A score of 85 or above is a portfolio-candidate threshold, subject to trusted review.

### 7. Dynamics & Expression

Mission: shape emotion through intentional vocal contrast. Lessons cover healthy soft/strong singing, gradual change, phrase emphasis, and contrasting interpretations. Practices compare three readings. The Core challenge is `Sing It Three Ways`. Rubric: dynamic control 25, healthy tone 20, phrasing 20, emotional contrast 20, artistic choice 15; pass 70.

### 8. Microphone & Recording Basics

Mission: work confidently with microphones, phones, and simple recording spaces. Lessons cover distance, angle, plosives, room noise, levels, and playback review without requiring paid equipment. The Core challenge is `Studio Ready Take`. Rubric: positioning 20, plosive control 15, level consistency 20, cleanliness 20, delivery 25; pass 70. Score 85+ is a portfolio candidate.

### 9. Performance Confidence

Mission: develop presence and recovery while performing. Lessons cover preparation, grounded focus, purposeful movement, audience connection, and continuing through small mistakes. The Core challenge is `One Take Performance`. Rubric: preparation 15, presence 25, expression/movement 20, recovery/continuity 25, audience connection 15; pass 70.

### 10. Beginner Showcase

Mission: integrate Beginner technique, musicianship, and storytelling in a polished performance. Lessons cover repertoire planning, technical run-through, and presentation readiness. The video brief models a safe final preparation sequence rather than promising performance perfection. Practices perform a readiness check and use a real review tool if assigned. The Core challenge is the Beginner showcase. Rubric: technique 30, accuracy/rhythm 25, expression/story 30, professionalism/completion 15; pass 70. Score 85+ is a portfolio candidate.

### Weaknesses found and refinements made

- Every lesson originally contained exactly three blocks. All 30 now include a fourth, skill-specific `Try This Now` block requiring physical, vocal, listening, recording, or performance action.
- Pitch instruction needed a clearer Beginner distinction between sharp and flat tendencies. Module 3 now asks students to observe whether a sustained or destination note consistently lands above or below the reference without turning the lesson into advanced theory.
- Module 9 mentioned preparation but needed a direct nerves-management behavior. Its quick practice now uses a timed grounding, breath, focus, intention, and start routine.
- Module 10 contained the correct capstone components but its complete sequence needed to be unmistakable. The three lessons now enact selection/planning, full dress rehearsal/evidence review, limited correction, final capture, reflection, and submission.
- Several activities use recording because singing evidence must be observable, but the experience is varied through physical resets, reference-note listening, clapping/counting, A/B tone experiments, listener communication, dynamic interpretation, room tests, camera presence, one-take recovery, and a studio-style finale.
- ARIA targets originally identified strengths and risks but did not consistently name the supporting evidence or priority rule. Every module now names at least four evidence targets and a specific highest-priority coaching rule. These are readiness definitions, not claims of implemented media analysis.

## ARIA and career governance

Each module stores evidence targets as structured JSON so ARIA can later recommend, explain, analyze, coach, summarize, predict, and personalize from allowed evidence. The authoring does not grant ARIA mutation authority: it cannot grant access, change roles, award XP or certificates, modify grades, complete lessons, or override instructor decisions. Career mission ideas are optional teacher-reviewed extensions, not enrollments, grades, or official records.
