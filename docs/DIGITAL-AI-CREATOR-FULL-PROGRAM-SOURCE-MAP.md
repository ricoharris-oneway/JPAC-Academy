## 5. Source cleanup findings

### Formatting and naming

- Decorative emoji and icon glyphs extract as unreadable replacement characters even though the rendered pages display recognizable icons. Strip decorative artifacts from rollout data.
- Normalize inconsistent `LEVEL 3 - ADVANCED` and `LEVEL 4 - MASTER` headings to `AI Creative Director` and `AI Production Master`.
- Preserve the numbered 1-48 sequence; do not promote midterm headings into extra modules.
- Normalize capitalization and punctuation while retaining recognizable source titles.
- Use consistent `Practice`, `Core Challenge`, `Reflection`, `Submission`, `Completion Criteria`, and `Mastery` labels.

### Assessment and workflow

- The PDF uses Tool Task, Tool-Based Skill Practice, Creative Application, Main Assignment/Main Creation Assignment, Submit, Professional Submission, Reflection, completion, and mastery language inconsistently.
- Convert external or generic submission language to the existing JPAC-native submission and teacher-review workflow.
- Preserve the source's academic intent, but map each module into one optional Practice, one required Core Challenge, and the canonical five-category rubric.
- The PDF's 100-point scoring models are assessment frameworks, not XP values.

### Product and platform volatility

- Google Flow, Gemini, Veo 3.1, Nano Banana, Flow Agent, and Gemini Omni are current ecosystem references rather than permanent curriculum anchors.
- Model capabilities, names, age requirements, availability, pricing, and interfaces can change.
- Teach prompting, visual direction, cinematography, continuity, storytelling, iteration, diagnosis, production documentation, and responsible delivery as the enduring competencies.
- All direct Google/AI exercises require catalog and platform-policy review before activation.

### Minor access and external accounts

- The source explicitly notes current 18+ Flow access limitations and recommends `Instructor-Guided AI Lab` for direct exercises involving students under 18.
- No learner may be required to create an external account, accept third-party terms, pay for a service, or independently use an age-restricted platform.
- Provide teacher-run demonstrations, supplied outputs, storyboard/written alternatives, and other accessible JPAC-native evidence paths.

### Media and instructional resources

- `Watch`, Google introductions, documentation, examples, and other instructional-resource references are not approved media records.
- Every instructional video/resource remains `MEDIA NEEDS REVIEW`.
- Do not create or activate media rows during draft rollout.

### AI safety, privacy, likeness, and consent

- Real-person likeness, voice, performance, personal images, private locations, and uploaded footage require explicit permission and privacy review.
- Do not encourage impersonation, deceptive media, non-consensual simulation, or misleading representations of real people.
- Do not upload private, sensitive, copyrighted, or unauthorized material.
- Minors must not independently contact unknown adults, clients, brands, or platform representatives.

### Copyright, IP, misinformation, and disclosure

- Music, visual references, characters, brands, scripts, footage, voices, and other inputs must be original, supplied, licensed, public-domain, or teacher approved.
- Preserve credits, provenance, permissions, and the human creative contribution.
- Require appropriate disclosure of AI involvement.
- Explicitly address deceptive media, misinformation, misleading commercial claims, and fabricated evidence.
- Never teach or imply platform-safeguard bypassing.

### Creative-process quality

- Reject excessive reliance on magic prompts, copied prompt recipes, or the assumption that longer prompts always fix failures.
- Require intent, planning, references, generation review, diagnosis, targeted revision, comparison, and reflection.
- Distinguish generated output from the student's creative direction and decision-making.

### Digital AI Creator rubric framework

Each criterion must include four non-empty performance bands. Assessment must reward human creative decisions and process evidence rather than model novelty or platform access.

## 8. Aria Digital AI Creator Tutor guidance

Aria functions as:

- Creative Director
- Prompt Coach
- Filmmaking Mentor
- AI Production Guide

Aria should:

- teach students to think like creators and directors;
- clarify intent before suggesting generation changes;
- diagnose failures before regenerating;
- identify prompt, reference, model-limit, continuity, direction, and workflow failures;
- protect character and environment continuity;
- distinguish AI output from human creative direction;
- preserve student originality and require documented decisions;
- teach filmmaking, storytelling, composition, lighting, camera, sound, editing, and production language;
- respect copyright, privacy, provenance, and platform policies;
- require permission for real people's likenesses and voices;
- encourage appropriate AI disclosure;
- recommend safe, private, instructor-guided, and accessible alternatives;
- compare revisions and explain whether they improved the intended result.

Aria must not:

- provide or encourage magic-prompt dependency;
- complete graded projects for students;
- imitate copyrighted works or living creators on demand;
- encourage impersonation, deceptive media, misinformation, or fabricated evidence;
- encourage copyright infringement or unauthorized uploads;
- bypass or teach students to bypass platform safeguards;
- direct minors to use age-restricted tools independently;
- assess, approve, publish, award XP, grant mastery, or unlock curriculum.
