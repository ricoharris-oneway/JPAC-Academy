## 5. Source cleanup findings

### Formatting and assessment labels

- Extracted text contains unreadable replacement glyphs around level, quiz, grading, certificate, and section headings. Normalize to clean UTF-8 text and standard punctuation.
- Several headings wrap across lines. Join wrapped title lines without changing academic meaning.
- The PDF states a standard 100-point regular-module structure: Tool-Based Skill Practice 30, Creative Application 20, Main Assignment 35, Artist Reflection 10, and Professional Submission 5.
- Individual modules inconsistently use `Tool-Based Task`, `Tool Task`, `Submit`, `Submit:`, `Reflection`, and sometimes omit displayed point values or professional-submission lines. Normalize roles and scoring without treating source points as XP.
- Missing reflection or professional-submission prompts may be added only as clearly identified rollout normalization requiring teacher review.

### External workflows, platforms, and tools

- Replace all generic, Google Classroom, or external submission language with the existing JPAC submission and teacher-review workflow.
- Streaming services, distributors, PROs, social platforms, analytics services, design tools, planning tools, and third-party business platforms are conceptual examples only until approved.
- Do not require external accounts, paid subscriptions, public profiles, public uploads, real registrations, real distribution, or real outreach.
- All named tools remain `NEEDS CATALOG REVIEW`; no tool or Lab binding is authorized.

### Media

- The source contains named videos and external instructional references across distribution, royalties, copyright, record deals, promotion, and other topics.
- Every video is `MEDIA NEEDS REVIEW`. Inclusion in the PDF does not establish availability, accuracy, accessibility, age suitability, neutrality, or rights clearance.
- Create no active media rows. Broken, outdated, contradictory, or unsuitable links remain inactive review notes.

### Legal, financial, and business boundaries

- Copyright, ownership, credits, split sheets, publishing, royalties, registrations, record deals, agreements, sponsorships, budgets, revenue, touring, and portfolio topics remain educational and review flagged.
- The course must not provide individualized legal, contract, tax, financial, or investment advice.
- Students must not sign or interpret real agreements, register rights accounts, negotiate real deals, incur expenses, or make financial commitments through assignments.
- Revenue and career examples must not promise money, fame, record deals, followers, sponsorships, or guaranteed outcomes.
- Mock budgets, agreements, registrations, campaigns, pitches, and release plans must be clearly fictional or simulated.

### Minor safety, privacy, and consent

- Social media and audience modules must protect identity, location, contact details, account credentials, images, likeness, and private communications.
- Minors must not contact unknown adults privately, publish personal information, or create public accounts as a course requirement.
- Networking, artist-team, media, sponsorship, partnership, touring, and collaboration activities require teacher mediation, approved participants, consent, professional boundaries, and documented contributions.
- Public-facing work must always have a private JPAC-native alternative.

## 8. Aria Music Business / Artist Development Tutor guidance

Aria may act as an artist manager, brand strategist, music-business mentor, and career coach. Aria should:

- teach strategy rather than shortcuts;
- explain terminology in age-appropriate language;
- reinforce ownership, permissions, credits, and contributor recognition;
- protect privacy, account security, personal information, and online safety;
- distinguish meaningful fans and community relationships from follower counts;
- encourage realistic planning, reflection, healthy boundaries, and sustainable work;
- use mock examples for agreements, budgets, registrations, negotiations, partnerships, and campaigns;
- scale independence, vocabulary, and strategic complexity by level.

Aria must not promise fame, deals, money, sponsorships, audience growth, or guaranteed outcomes. Aria must not provide individualized legal, contract, tax, investment, or financial advice; tell students to sign agreements; encourage minors to contact unknown adults privately; assess final work; approve publication; award XP; grant mastery; unlock modules; or issue certificates.
