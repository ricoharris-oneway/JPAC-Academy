## 5. Source cleanup findings

### Formatting and naming

- Extracted headings contain unreadable replacement glyphs and encoding artifacts around level, quiz, and certificate labels. Rollout content should use clean UTF-8 text and standard punctuation.
- Module titles frequently wrap across lines in the PDF; the canonical titles above join those lines without changing meaning.
- The source alternates between `Tool-Based Task` and `Tool Task`. Normalize the curriculum role to **Optional Practice Activity** while preserving the source activity instructions in review notes.
- `Submit`, `Submit:`, and similar variants are inconsistent. Replace all with explicit JPAC-native evidence instructions.
- Some modules show the standard point labels (`Tool-Based Skill Practice` 30, `Creative Application` 20, `Main Assignment` 35, `Creator Reflection` 10, `Professional Submission` 5), while other module sections omit point values or labels. Treat the PDF's 100-point framework as assessment scoring, not XP, and normalize each Core Challenge to the approved five-category rubric before SQL review.
- Reflection and professional-submission language is uneven. Each rollout Core Challenge should include a short creator reflection and submission-completeness expectation when source-compatible; additions must be identified as normalization, not quoted source content.

### External workflows and tools

- Replace every external or generic submission instruction with the existing JPAC submission workflow. Do not preserve Google Classroom or other third-party classroom submission behavior.
- BandLab is presented as an entry DAW and the source also mentions GarageBand, Logic Pro, FL Studio, Ableton Live, and other approved tools. These are optional examples only until catalog approval.
- Cakewalk, CapCut, plugins, browser DAWs, and similar software must not become mandatory requirements. Provide tool-neutral and no-cost/accessibility alternatives.
- Do not require creation of external accounts, public uploads, social posting, or paid software subscriptions without separate JPAC approval and consent controls.

### Media

- The PDF contains many YouTube URLs and named tutorials across DAW operation, drums, bass, melody, songwriting, collaboration, mixing, copyright, publishing, and delivery.
- Every URL is `MEDIA NEEDS REVIEW`. No URL is approved, trusted, current, accessible, age-appropriate, or rights-cleared merely because it appears in the PDF.
- The rollout must create no active instructional-media rows. Broken, unavailable, placeholder, contradictory, or unsuitable links remain inactive review notes.

### Rights, originality, and collaboration

- Hook, genre, producer-identity, signature-sound, songwriting, and artist-study work must distinguish analysis and inspiration from imitation. Students must create original evidence.
- Credits, split sheets, publishing revenue, catalogs, and portfolios require `RIGHTS/LEGAL REVIEW`; educational examples are not legal or financial advice.
- Collaboration and producing-for-an-artist modules require approved participants, age-appropriate communication, consent, contribution records, credits, and privacy safeguards for minors.
- Student recordings, vocals, names, likenesses, and portfolio materials must not be publicly shared by default.

## 8. Aria Music Production Tutor guidance

Aria may act as a producer mentor, songwriting coach, studio guide, and critical-listening partner. Aria should:

- protect originality and student voice;
- coach decisions rather than create submitted work for the student;
- distinguish intentional creative choices from technical errors;
- ask focused questions about concept, rhythm, melody, harmony, arrangement, recording, and mix decisions;
- encourage completion, revision, reflection, and professional file organization;
- reinforce credits, ownership, splits, consent, and respectful collaboration;
- scale vocabulary, independence, and production expectations by level;
- offer tool-neutral and accessible alternatives.

Aria remains advisory only. It must not perform final assessment, approve work, award XP, grant mastery, unlock modules, publish content, or replace teacher judgment.
