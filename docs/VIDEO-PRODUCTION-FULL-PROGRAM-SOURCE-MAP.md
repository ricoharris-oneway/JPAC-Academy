## 5. Source conflicts and human decisions required

1. **Master numbering:** Decide whether 20 and 24 are intentional Program identifiers, misplaced supplemental modules, or numbering errors. Until approved, preserve them as evidenced numbers and do not fabricate gaps.
2. **Capstone placement:** Module 10 is explicitly the Master capstone, yet Modules 20 and 24 follow it in the PDF. Decide whether those are post-capstone extensions or should be held out.
3. **Intermediate color repetition:** Modules 3, 5, and 11 cover color correction/grading. Define distinct scopes or approve consolidation; do not silently delete them.
4. **Advanced color repetition:** Modules 3 and 11 overlap, and both repeat Level 2 material. Define progressive outcomes before SQL.
5. **VFX progression:** Advanced Module 8, Master Module 3, and Master Module 20 overlap; “VFX Basics” at 20 appears out of sequence.
6. **Festival/distribution repetition:** Master Modules 5, 8, and 24 overlap in festival, financing, submission, and distribution content.
7. **Tutor mismatch:** The Tutor describes its own numbered topic outlines, including a Master “Master Film Production Project,” but these are coaching groupings only and cannot replace the Program inventory.

## 6. Recommended draft rollout model

For each human-approved Program module:

- three source-aligned draft lessons;
- one optional draft Practice activity where appropriate, with 0 XP and bonus XP role;
- one required draft Core Challenge with `activity_type = performance`, 350 XP, core XP role, passing score 70, and resubmission allowed;
- one five-category rubric totaling exactly 100;
- Aria coaching as advisory support only;
- accessible no-tool, no-specialized-equipment, supplied-footage, or storyboard alternative;
- no publication, student access, active media, tool binding, or Career Path attachment.

Recommended rubric (20 points each):

1. Story & Concept Development
2. Camera / Visual Technique
3. Editing & Pacing
4. Sound / Audio-Visual Integration
5. Production Readiness & Professional Presentation

Rubric language must allow equivalent accessible evidence and must not require paid software, specialized cameras, drones, performers, or public distribution.

## 7. Aria Video Production Mentor guidance

Aria may coach story clarity, shot composition, camera movement, lighting intent and safety, editing rhythm and pacing, sound design, color correction/grading, production planning, and portfolio/reel reflection. Guidance should scale from plain beginner language to professional vocabulary and should encourage iterative review.

Aria must:

- offer concrete, supportive, technically accurate feedback;
- recommend accessible alternatives and supplied assets;
- remind learners about consent, location safety, drone law, copyright, music/media rights, privacy, and distribution permissions;
- distinguish creative suggestion from required assessment criteria;
- remain advisory and never assess, approve, award XP, grant mastery, publish, or unlock curriculum.
